One-neuron personas · follow-up

Who reads the persona neuron's write?

A trained persona neuron fires hardest on a few chat-template tokens, and keeping it only there keeps most of the persona. So something must carry what it writes at those positions into the reply. Here is a quick first look at which attention heads do.

What I measured

For each of the 23 trained neurons (5 owl neurons at layer 5, and 3 neurons each for six other personas at layer 8), I took 32 of its own eval chats, prompt plus its reply, and ran them twice:

  • through plain Qwen3-8B, and
  • with the trained neuron switched on only at the prompt's template tokens.

For every attention head in layers 9–35, the carry score is how much of that difference at the template positions the head moves into the reply tokens: its attention from reply to template, times the change in its values there, through its output projection. Deeper layers have larger activations, so each head is scored relative to the mean of its own layer.

A control, on one run each of five personas: at the same template positions, add a change of the same size per token but in a fixed random direction, and score the heads the same way.

What this does and doesn't show

It points to a shared set of reader heads that pull template information into the reply, and persona writes that the model carries much more strongly than an arbitrary change. It is correlational and first-order: the score also counts whatever the layers in between did to the write, and it does not show these heads are needed. The direct test is to block attention from the reply to the template in just these heads and check that the personas fade while plain Qwen stays normal.
